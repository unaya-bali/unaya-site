How much does a villa in Bali earn? An honest calculation

By the Unaya team · Updated 16 November 2026 · 6 min read

You often read “15 to 20% yield a year”. The reality is more modest, but still attractive when the villa is well located and well run. Here is the full calculation, with a real price example, leaving nothing out.

In this article
  1. Gross income: nights booked × average rate
  2. What goes out before it reaches you
  3. The result: net income
  4. Pitfalls to avoid

Gross income: nights booked × average rate

Everything starts with two numbers: the average nightly rate and the number of nights booked in the year. Take a well-located 2- or 3-bedroom villa in south Bali, rented at IDR 3,000,000 a night on average, booked 65% of the year: 237 nights.

Gross income: 237 × 3,000,000 = IDR 711 million a year.

What goes out before it reaches you

  • Platforms (Airbnb, Booking…) take around 15% on average: about IDR 107 million. Less if some bookings come direct through your website.
  • Tax on nightly stays (PBJT): 10% in Badung regency, included in the displayed rate. About IDR 65 million.
  • Management: at Unaya, 18% calculated on what remains after platforms and tax, about IDR 97 million. Careful: some companies calculate their commission on gross, which changes the result a lot.
  • Running costs: staff, electricity, water, internet, pool and garden upkeep, linen, small repairs, and a reserve to replace furniture. Allow about 20% of gross: IDR 142 million.

The result: net income

711 − 107 − 65 − 97 − 142 = about IDR 300 million a year, i.e. IDR 25 million a month, or roughly USD 16,500 a year.

If the villa cost USD 250,000, the net yield is about 6.6% a year, before income tax. That is within the range market studies give for a well-run villa once established: 6 to 10%.

Pitfalls to avoid

  • Promises of 15–20%: they often forget commissions, tax and the low months.
  • The first year: it takes time to build reviews and find your place. Yield is usually lower at the start.
  • Lease length: on a leasehold, the villa loses value as the remaining years shrink. A good yield on a short lease can be a poor investment.
  • The licence: since 2026, listings without a licence can be removed from platforms. A villa you cannot rent earns nothing.

What really lifts income

Location (near a beach, a surf spot, a lively area), photos, prices adjusted every day to demand, how fast you reply to guests, and reviews. A villa rated 9.8/10 rents for more, and more often, than one at 8.5.

Key points
  • Net income ≈ 40–50% of gross, before taxes.
  • Realistic yield for a well-run villa: 6–10% a year.
  • Check what base the management commission is calculated on.
  • Remaining lease and licence matter as much as the nightly rate.
