Villa management in Bali: what a manager does, and what it costs

By the Unaya team · Updated 16 November 2026 · 6 min read

Renting out a villa in Bali from abroad is a real job: guests at any hour, staff to supervise, taxes, breakdowns. A management company does it for you. Here is what it does, what it really costs and how to compare offers.

What a management company does

  • Marketing: listings on Airbnb, Booking, Expedia and the villa’s website, photos, copy, and above all pricing, adjusted every day to season and demand.
  • Guests: replies before the stay, check-in, 24/7 support, check-out, and reviews.
  • The villa: staff (cleaning, garden, pool), linen, basic supplies, maintenance and repairs.
  • The numbers: collecting payments, paying the tax on nightly stays (PBJT), expenses with receipts and a report every month.

How much does it cost?

Most companies charge a commission on rental income. For full management in Bali you often see around 20%; some lighter online offers are around 15%.

At Unaya: full management from 18%, or marketing only at 11% if you run the villa on site. No fixed fees, no set-up fee.

The trap: the calculation base

Two companies at 18% do not cost the same. On IDR 10,000,000 of bookings, a commission on gross is IDR 1,800,000. Calculated after platforms (≈ 15%) and the 10% tax, as at Unaya, it is about IDR 1,366,000.

Always ask: is the commission on gross, or after platforms and tax? And what is billed on top (linen, photos, small works)?

What to check in the contract

  • Length and notice: can you stop easily if you are not happy?
  • The monthly report: bookings, income, expenses with receipts, and access to an online portal.
  • Who sets prices, and your right to use the villa yourself.
  • Expenses: above what amount the company must ask for your approval.
  • The licence: who officially rents out the villa? Since May 2026 a new PT PMA can no longer get a tourist-accommodation licence in Bali, so going through an already licensed management company often becomes the solution.

Manage it yourself, or with a company?

Doing it yourself saves the commission, but means answering guests at night in several languages, guessing prices, hiring and supervising staff, paying taxes and dealing with a broken pool pump on a Saturday night. From abroad, it rarely works.

A good management company usually pays for itself through better income: more nights booked, better rates and better reviews.

Key points
  • Full management: often 15–20% in the market; Unaya from 18%.
  • Check the commission’s calculation base.
  • Insist on a monthly report with receipts.
  • In 2026, the licence to rent out is a key point.
