Define your project

It all starts with your goal: it decides the location, the budget and the legal structure.

Living there

You spend more than six months a year in Bali. Peace and quiet matter more than being close to the beaches: a house among the rice fields, a little out of the way, often gives you more space for the same budget.

A second home that earns

You come for a few weeks a year and rent it out the rest of the time. The location must appeal to travellers: close to beaches, restaurants and sought-after areas such as Uluwatu / Jimbaran, Tanah Lot / Canggu West or Seminyak / Kerobokan.

A rental investment

Your priority is yield. Two strategies: an established area, safer but more expensive to buy, or an up-and-coming one, more affordable, with room for capital growth.

02

Freehold or leasehold: what the law allows

In Indonesia, freehold land title (Hak Milik) is reserved for Indonesian citizens. Foreigners have several legal options:

Leasehold

The most common: you lease the land for a long period, often 25 to 30 years, with an extension option negotiated from the start. Usually the most profitable route for a rental project.

A PT PMA company

An Indonesian company with foreign capital can hold a long-term, renewable right to build (Hak Guna Bangunan, HGB). It requires significant investment capital and rigorous accounting.

Right of use

A foreigner with a residence permit may, under conditions, hold a right of use (Hak Pakai) on a home for their own residence.

Avoid at all costs: nominee arrangements (an Indonesian buying in their name on your behalf). They offer no legal protection and can cost you your property.

03

Buy a villa or build one

Buying an existing villa

Faster: the villa can be rented out almost straight away. Titles, permits and the condition of the building must be checked carefully.

Building

Often the best return: a villa designed for renting, to your taste, on well-chosen land. The project takes longer — finding the land, plans, building permit, construction, furnishing — and needs on-site follow-up.

In both cases, an Indonesian notary (PPAT) must draw up the deeds, and every document must be checked before any payment.

04

In your own name or through a company?

In your own name

Simpler and cheaper to set up. Suited to a home or a small rental project, as long as rental income is properly declared.

With a PT PMA

More paperwork and running costs, but real advantages: holding the right to build, a possible investor residence permit (KITAS), a clear framework to receive rents, pay staff and depreciate investments. Worth it above a certain level of rental income.

The right choice depends on your budget, your tax residence and your expected income: we put you in touch with trusted legal and tax advisers to confirm it.

05

Tax numbers and taxes

  • NPWP: the national tax number. A company receives one automatically; a foreign individual can obtain one under conditions (usually with a residence permit).
  • Accommodation tax: short-term rentals are subject to a regional tax (10% in Badung), paid by guests and passed on to the region. The villa must be registered with the regional tax office.
  • Land and building tax (PBB): due every year on the land and the building.
  • Income tax: rental profits are taxable; the rate and form depend on the structure chosen (individual, company, dividends) and on tax treaties with your country.

Tax rules change regularly: the information on this page is general and must be confirmed by an adviser before any decision.
