Owning property as a foreigner

Can a foreigner fully own land in Bali?

No. Freehold (Hak Milik) is reserved for Indonesian citizens. A foreigner can instead lease land long-term (leasehold), hold a right of use (Hak Pakai) under conditions, or invest through a foreign-owned company (PT PMA).[1]

What is leasehold (Hak Sewa)?

A long-term lease signed before a notary with the Indonesian landowner: often 20 to 30 years, with an extension option to be written into the contract. You can build, rent out and resell the remaining lease. Key points to put in writing: term, extension price, right to sublet and assign, what happens to the building at the end. Lease payments are subject to a 10% final tax (PPh 4(2)).[11]

What is Hak Pakai for a foreigner?

A right to use a residential house, open to foreigners holding a residence permit, to live in (not to run a business). It is granted for a limited term, extendable and renewable. A minimum price applies: in Bali, IDR 5 billion for a landed house.[1][2]

What is a PT PMA and when do you need one?

An Indonesian foreign-owned company. As a foreigner, you need one to run a villa as a tourist rental. Since BKPM Regulation 5/2025 (October 2025), the minimum paid-up capital is IDR 2.5 billion per business line; the total planned investment remains IDR 10 billion, and land and construction can count for accommodation. The company can hold a right to build (Hak Guna Bangunan, HGB).[3][1]

Can I buy in an Indonesian friend's name (nominee)?

Avoid it. The property legally belongs to the nominee, you have no protection, and in late 2025 Bali passed a bylaw explicitly banning nominee arrangements, with sanctions including for notaries who facilitate them.[5]

Land and zoning

What do the zoning colours mean?

Each plot has a use set by the spatial plan (RTRW / RDTR): agricultural and green zones (often green / yellow), residential zones, tourism and commercial zones. A villa rented to tourists must be in a zone that allows tourist accommodation.

How do I check a plot's zoning?

Before committing: ask for the land certificate (SHM, HGB…) and have the notary check it at the land office (BPN), check the plot's use in the detailed plan (RDTR) and the land-use conformity (KKPR) through the OSS system.[4]

Can you still build a villa on a rice field?

Less and less. Bali banned the conversion of farmland (Governor's instruction), then passed a provincial bylaw in late 2025 controlling the conversion of productive land and banning nominees. Exceptions mainly cover public projects and local owners' own homes, not commercial villas.[6][5]

Building permits and rental licence

Which permits do I need to build?

The PBG (building approval, which replaced the old IMB) before works, then the SLF (certificate of worthiness) once the building is finished, under Government Regulation 16/2021. A villa without PBG and SLF cannot get a rental licence.[7]

Which licence do I need to rent to tourists?

An accommodation business registered in the OSS system: business ID (NIB) with the right activity code (KBLI). For a foreigner this goes through a PT PMA; some forms of accommodation (such as pondok wisata, rooms in a local home) are reserved for Indonesians. You also need tax registrations (NPWP, and NPWPD with the regency for PBJT).[4][9]

What happens without a licence?

In 2026 the Ministry of Tourism started bringing villas and accommodation listed on platforms into line: listings without verified NIB/KBLI can be removed from Airbnb, Booking.com, etc. (deadline pushed back to 31 May 2026). Unlicensed villas have also been sealed off.[8][9]

4

Taxes

What tax applies to nightly stays?

PBJT (tax on accommodation services): 10% of the room price in Badung regency (Uluwatu, Canggu, Seminyak…), villas included. It is usually included in the displayed price and paid monthly to the regency.[10]

And income tax on rental income?

It depends on the structure. A PT PMA pays corporate income tax on its profit (standard rate 22%, reduced regimes possible for small companies), and dividends paid abroad bear withholding tax (reduced by tax treaties). An individual renting out land or a building pays a 10% final tax on the rent.[11] Have your case checked by an Indonesian accountant.

Which taxes apply when buying?

The seller pays a 2.5% final tax on the sale price; the buyer pays transfer duty (BPHTB), up to 5%, set by the regency. These apply to transfers of rights (freehold, HGB), not to a simple lease.[12]

Purchase and construction costs

How much does it cost to build a villa?

Market ranges for 2026 (excluding land and furniture): about USD 850–1,100/m² for a standard villa, USD 1,200–1,600/m² for upscale, USD 1,800/m² and more for luxury. These are agency ranges, not official figures: only a detailed quote counts.[14]

And leasehold land?

Prices vary hugely with area, access and view. In sought-after southern areas (Berawa, Bingin…), listings often show IDR 25–60 million per are (100 m²) per year. Also budget for the notary, legal due diligence, permits, setting up the PT PMA if needed, and furniture.[14]

Realistic returns

How is a villa's net income calculated?

Gross income = nights booked × average nightly rate. Then deduct platform commissions (≈ 15%), PBJT (10%), management fees, staff, electricity, maintenance and a furniture reserve. Usually 40 to 50% of gross income remains before income tax.[13]

What return can I expect?

2026 market studies put the net yield of a well-managed villa at 6 to 10% a year once stabilised (year 2-3), and noticeably less in year one. Be wary of 15-20% promises: they often ignore commissions, taxes and low months.[13]

What does the notary do?

The notary (Notaris, and PPAT for land-right transfers) checks the certificate at the land office (BPN), drafts and registers the deed (notarial lease or deed of sale), and calculates and collects transfer taxes. This is not a full due diligence: zoning, permits, access, neighbours and disputes must be checked as well, ideally with an independent lawyer.
